## Structure

- Abstract needs background, gap, contribution/problem, method, completed results, and bounded implication. Keep it accessible to a beginner-level ML reader. Do not introduce unexplained custom metrics or all historical diagnostics.
- Introduction compresses the paper. Move from general reliability to the exact symbolic task, closest work/gap, approach, evaluation setup, two or three important numbers, and three or four full-sentence contributions.
- Background/related work needs two or three coherent clusters. Each has a survey paragraph and a two-to-four-sentence differentiation paragraph. Acknowledge close and concurrent work. Do not claim novelty merely because related work was omitted.
- Methodology describes the whole input-to-output process, exact prompt/control choices, sample construction, scoring, resource bounds, and why choices fit the question. Separate implemented procedures from completed evidence.
- Results are organized by research question. Each visual needs a primary takeaway and bounded secondary observations, usually within two paragraphs. Give numerators/denominators. Reference every table and figure in prose.
- Discussion separates evidence from interpretation, plausible alternatives, limitations, and future work. Failed trials normally belong in an appendix unless they motivate a demonstrated methodological decision.
- Conclusion summarizes completed work in past tense, usually in one or two paragraphs. It introduces no new findings or unrun experiment as an accomplishment.

## Style and author verification

Use standard technical English, complete sentences, active voice, simple words, and defined technical terms. Split sentences that become difficult to follow, often above roughly 25 words. Avoid em/en dashes and semicolons as prose connectors. Avoid inflated contribution language. Check all prose, numbers, equations, citations, and visuals manually before submission.

Verify every reference's author names, title, year, venue, DOI, and pages. Prefer canonical published proceedings to a preprint when a corresponding publication exists. A workshop is not a main-track conference publication. An accepted preprint needs verified final metadata before inventing a proceedings citation.

Use the authentic current template for the chosen venue and check its actual rules. Guidance examples for other venues do not establish this paper's deadline, page limit, anonymity requirements, or submission checklist. Clear hierarchy, legible visuals, consistent citations, no unresolved references, and explicit limitations matter independently of venue.
